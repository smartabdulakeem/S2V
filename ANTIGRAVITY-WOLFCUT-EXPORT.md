## What this is for

The owner picks images in Smart-Studio, renders, and then wants to open the result in
**WolfCut** (`https://github.com/jub0t/WolfCut`, MPL-2.0) as a real editing timeline — to trim,
adjust the audio, add music and sound effects, and nudge individual pictures.

He is **not** getting a flat video to trim. He is getting **his film taken apart**: every picture
its own block at its own real duration, narration on its own track, captions as text clips. That is
the whole point. A single flattened MP4 would be useless to him.

**Nothing in this brief copies WolfCut code.** We write a JSON file in their documented format.
That is not copying and carries no licence obligation. Do not vendor, fork, or paste any WolfCut
source into this repo.

## Why this is small

Three facts, all verified in the code — do not re-derive them, but do not contradict them either:

1. **The orchestrator already builds the timeline.** At `pipeline/orchestrator.py` ~296 (Stage B,
   "Building Timeline"), it has `audio_paths_map[seg_id]` and `durations_map[seg_id]` for every
   segment, measured with `ffprobe` from the real narration. That is the hook point.
2. **The narration survives the render.** `_cleanup_intermediates()` at `orchestrator.py` ~119
   deletes video intermediates and `segment_*_mixed_audio.wav`, but its docstring is explicit:
   *"Narration MP3s and SRTs stay."* So the audio the timeline points at is still on disk afterwards.
3. **Durations are already solved.** `resolve_shot_durations(shots, total_segment_duration)` in
   `pipeline/validator.py` ~152 splits a segment's spoken length across its shots. `composer.py` ~866
   already calls it. Reuse it. Do not invent a second duration rule.

## The one design decision, already made

**Export after the render, not before.** Do not move narration generation earlier, and do not touch
when audio is made. That change belongs to a later piece of work. Export at the point where the
render already knows every duration.

## Job 1 — write the document

Add `pipeline/wolfcut_export.py` with one entry point:

```python
def write_wolfcut_project(script_data: dict, audio_paths_map: dict, durations_map: dict,
                          project_dir: str) -> str
```

It writes `<project_dir>/<slug>.wolfcut` and returns the path.

**Read the format from the source before writing a line of it.** The authoritative definitions are:

- `engine/crates/wolfcut-project/src/lib.rs` — the saved document wrapper and `DOCUMENT_VERSION`
- `desktop/src/lib/generated/Project.ts`, `Timeline.ts`, `Track.ts`, `Clip.ts`, `MediaItem.ts`,
  `ClipKind.ts`, `MediaKind.ts`, `TextStyle.ts`

These are generated by `ts-rs` from the Rust structs, so they are exact. Pin the commit you read
them at and record it in a comment at the top of the module. Field names are camelCase.

What is known so far, to be confirmed against those files, not trusted from this brief:

- `Project` holds `media[]`, `fonts[]`, `timelines[]`, `activeTimelineId`. The saved document wraps
  it with `wolfcut`, `version`, `name`, `video`.
- `Timeline` holds `id`, `name`, `tracks[]`, `clips[]`. The founding timeline id is `"TL1"`.
- `Clip` requires `id`, `trackId`, `mediaId`, `name`, `kind`, `start`, `duration`, `sourceStart`,
  `volume`, `fadeIn`, `fadeOut`, `scale`, `offsetX`, `offsetY`, `rotation`, `opacity`, `speed`,
  `preservePitch`, `filters[]`, `videoEffects[]`. Times are **f64 seconds**, ids are **strings**.
- `MediaItem` requires `id`, `path`, `name`, `duration`, `kind`, `width`, `height`, `frameRate`,
  `frameRateFraction`, `videoCodec`, `audioCodec`, `hasAudio`.

**Three tracks, top to bottom:** pictures, narration, captions.

### The rule that will be got wrong

`plan_image_budget()` merges segments into runs when the image count is reduced. Shots that do not
own a picture carry **`share_with`** pointing at the shot that does.

**A clip must exist per distinct picture, not per shot.** Consecutive shots sharing one image
collapse into **one** clip whose duration is their durations added together. A 200-shot film reduced
to 40 images produces **40 picture clips**, not 200.

This is the same rule as `image_prompts.txt`, and the two must agree: **picture *n* in the timeline,
prompt *n* in `image_prompts.txt`, and `n.jpg` are the same thing.** If they disagree, the export is
wrong, whichever one is out.

Narration: one audio clip per segment, laid end to end, `start` being the running sum of the
preceding segments' durations. Captions: read the existing SRT via `parse_srt()` in
`pipeline/captions.py` ~249 and emit one text clip per cue at its own start and duration.

Paths must be absolute, because WolfCut opens the file from its own working directory.

## Tests

- A 60-segment script reduced to 12 images produces **12 picture clips**, not 60.
- Picture clip *n*, prompt line *n* in `image_prompts.txt`, and `n.jpg` name the same shot —
  asserted together in one test.
- Clip `start` times are contiguous: each clip starts exactly where the previous one ends, and the
  last clip ends at the total narration duration, within 1ms.
- Narration clips laid end to end sum to the same total.
- Every `mediaId` on a clip exists in `media[]`, and every `trackId` exists in `tracks[]`.
- Every media `path` is absolute and exists on disk.
- The document round-trips through `json.loads(json.dumps(doc))` unchanged.
- A film with no captions still produces a valid document.
