# Premium War & Conflict Prompts — Early Islamic History

For **manual generation** in Google Flow, Imagen, or any premium image model. These are the
shots that carry your war episodes, so they are worth generating by hand rather than in bulk.

Save every result straight into:

```
C:\Users\HomePC\Documents\GitHub\S2V\library\images\
```

Any filename. CLIP indexes by pixels, not names.

---

## 1. The style block — append to EVERY prompt

```
Shot on 35mm film, cinematic documentary photography, natural directional light,
shallow depth of field, muted earth palette of ochre sand, dust grey and deep
indigo shadow, fine film grain, historically accurate 7th century Arabian
Peninsula, early Islamic era.
```

## 2. The negative block — append after the style block

```
No modern objects, no firearms, no curved scimitars, no Ottoman or Persian
costume, no plate armour, no European castles, no text, no watermark, no
signature, no logo, no lens flare, no plastic-looking skin.
```

**Why these specific negatives:** models default to a generic "Middle East" look that is
centuries wrong. Curved scimitars and Ottoman dress are 800+ years too late. Plate armour
is European. Getting these out is the difference between a history channel and a fantasy one.

## 3. Period accuracy — what "correct" looks like

| Correct for the 7th century | Model will wrongly default to |
|---|---|
| **Straight double-edged swords** carried on a shoulder baldric | Curved scimitars |
| Mail shirts (*dir'*), leather lamellar, simple conical helmets | Plate armour, ornate helms |
| Round wooden or hide shields | Kite shields, heraldic devices |
| Plain black, white or red banners, no emblem | Elaborate heraldry, crescents |
| Undyed wool and linen robes, head wraps | Silk, embroidery, turbans with jewels |
| Arabian horses, camels, no horseshoes | Heavy European warhorses |
| Mud brick, palm trunk, stone | Domes, minarets, tilework |

Minarets and large domes are **later** architecture. Early mosques were low, flat-roofed and
plain.

## 4. One production note on figures

Depicting the Prophet ﷺ and, for many audiences, the Rashidun caliphs, is avoided in Islamic
tradition. It costs you nothing visually to work around it, and it protects the channel.

**The prompts below use these devices deliberately:**
- Backs turned, or figures seen from behind
- Silhouettes against light
- Hands, feet, hems of robes — detail rather than face
- Crowds where no single face resolves
- The empty space a person just left

This also happens to solve the AI-face problem. **Silhouettes and turned backs are what these
models render best.** Accuracy, respect, and technical reliability point the same way.

# How to use these

**Priority order for war episodes.** If you only generate 30, take these:
`A1 A4 B2 B5 C1 C2 C4 C5 D1 D2 D4 E1 E2 E3 E6 F1 F2 F3 F6 F7 G2 G3 H1 H3 I1 I3 I4 I6 C6 D6`

That set covers the full arc — call to arms, march, the clash, aftermath, cost — with enough
variety that no shot repeats within an episode.

**Generate 2–3 variations of each.** Different seeds give you cutting options, and shot
rhythm is what keeps a 24-minute film watchable.

**Vary the lighting** on the ones you use most. The same subject at dawn, at dusk, and in
storm light reads as three different shots.

**Prefer wide and silhouette when a figure is involved.** It is both more respectful and more
reliable — the models handle shapes far better than faces.
